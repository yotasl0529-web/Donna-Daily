Full story, sources & checks5 sources

The Federal Aviation Administration halted departures across the U.S. on Friday afternoon after what officials called a telecommunications failure, then reopened the airspace at 8:15 p.m. ET. Airlines were still working through thousands of delays and cancellations on Saturday. The FAA announced the stop at 3:54 p.m. ET “due to extraordinary circumstances,” and the BBC called it the longest nationwide ground stop since the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks; FlightAware counted more than 8,500 delayed and nearly 8,000 cancelled flights on Friday. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed an “incredibly old air traffic control system,” saying routers “got overloaded with data” and gave inconsistent readings, and warned the knock-on effects would last days. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of a cyberattack — though, NPR noted, he could not rule one out entirely — and that the outage was not related to Thursday’s Microsoft outage. CBS News, citing multiple sources, said an update to the private network connecting the air traffic control system caused it to reboot. As of early Saturday FlightAware showed more than 6,200 delays and 3,400 cancellations, CNBC said; the SEC moved some college football games to Sunday, and NBA and NHL teams scrambled to travel. Air Canada also cancelled U.S.–Canada flights, and Hong Kong’s Airport Authority said four flights between the city and the U.S. were cancelled and 13 delayed, with delays expected until Sunday.

Note: The cause is the administration’s preliminary account; investigators were still working out exactly what failed. The report about a network update rests on unnamed sources cited by CBS News. Flight counts are FlightAware tallies at different times; outlets described the stop as lasting “more than four hours” (BBC) or “about five hours” (CBS).
