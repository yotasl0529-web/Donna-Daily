Official statementUnnamed sourcesSpaceflight Now, Ars Technica and 2 more · Nov 15

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral, Florida, on Sunday, losing all 40 satellites it was carrying for Amazon’s Leo broadband network in the company’s first outright mission failure.

More4 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisAmazon is racing SpaceX’s Starlink, and its main launch provider is now grounded for an unknown period. With the Pentagon also relying on Vulcan, the loss deepens U.S. dependence on SpaceX at a time when competition in launch was supposed to be growing.

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral, Florida, on Sunday, losing all 40 satellites it was carrying for Amazon’s Leo broadband network in the company’s first outright mission failure. ULA, a Boeing–Lockheed Martin joint venture formed in 2006, said the vehicle “suffered an anomaly” and that it did not yet have data on the cause. Ars Technica, citing unnamed sources, reported that the problem occurred in the booster stage rather than the upper stage or strap-on boosters. It was Vulcan’s fifth flight and its first in about nine months, after separate investigations into its solid rocket boosters and its BE-4 main engines, and the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has booked for its planned constellation of more than 3,200 satellites. The failure is expected to ground the rocket, which also carries about half of the U.S. military’s national-security launches in the coming years, Ars reported.

Shares of New World Development fell more than 45% at Monday’s open to HK$2.655, an all-time low since its 1980 listing, after the heavily indebted developer proposed raising about HK$24.9 billion through a deeply discounted rights issue. The plan offers 11 new shares for every share held at HK$0.90 each, 81.5% below Friday’s close of HK$4.87, according to a company filing reported by RTHK. About a third of the proceeds would prepay 10% of roughly HK$82 billion in bank loans, with the rest of those loans extended by two years, and another third would repay other debt. Controlling shareholder Chow Tai Fook Enterprises, which owns about 45.3%, has committed to take up its full entitlement, and the rest is underwritten. JP Morgan said the discount was likely beyond most investors’ expectations and that the money would ease, but not end, the company’s liquidity stress.

Official statementNPR, NASA Science and 1 more · Nov 15

NASA says Voyager 1, launched in 1977, will reach a distance of one light-day from Earth — about 16 billion miles — at 5:16:07 a.m. EST on Wednesday, Nov. 18, and is inviting people to flash its call sign at the sky on Tuesday night.

File photo · Photo: NASA/JPL · Public domain (US government work)

More3 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisA nearly 50-year-old spacecraft still sending data from interstellar space is one of engineering’s great endurance stories, and the milestone is an easy invitation for anyone to take part — no telescope required.

NASA says Voyager 1, launched in 1977, will reach a distance of one light-day from Earth — about 16 billion miles — at 5:16:07 a.m. EST on Wednesday, Nov. 18, and is inviting people to flash its call sign at the sky on Tuesday night. A light-day is the distance light travels in 24 hours, so a command sent to the probe will take a day to arrive and another day for a reply to return. For “Shine Bright for Voyager’s Light-Day,” NASA is asking people to blink “VGR” in Morse code with phones or flashlights after sunset on Nov. 17. Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter and Saturn, became the most distant human-made object in 1998 and entered interstellar space in 2012. NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory plans a public discussion with mission members on Nov. 19.

BBC News, Al Jazeera · Nov 15–16

Martine Moïse, who was wounded in the 2021 attack that killed her husband, President Jovenel Moïse, appeared on Sunday on the electoral council’s list of 33 registered presidential candidates.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisA first vote in a decade would be a key step toward restoring an elected government in a country where armed gangs control much of the capital. Moïse’s candidacy brings the unresolved 2021 assassination back into the campaign — but the July date still depends on security.

Martine Moïse, who was wounded in the 2021 attack that killed her husband, President Jovenel Moïse, appeared on Sunday on the electoral council’s list of 33 registered presidential candidates. Other registered candidates include former presidents Michel Martelly and Jocelerme Privert and former prime minister Claude Joseph. Haiti last held a presidential election in 2016; elections planned for December were postponed last month because of insecurity, and a first-round vote is now set for July. Moïse has not publicly commented on her registration, but wrote on X last week that “Haiti will rise again on its own two feet, because politics needs to be done differently.”

BBC Sport, Al Jazeera · Nov 15

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisAndreeva’s dominance makes her a clear challenger for No. 1 next season. The final was also another reminder of how the war in Ukraine still shapes the women’s tour.

Russia’s Mirra Andreeva beat Ukraine’s Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in Indian Wells, California, on Sunday to win the WTA Finals, the event’s youngest champion in 22 years. The French Open champion did not drop a set all week and lost only 26 games in five matches, the BBC reported. She rises to No. 2 in the world, 293 points behind Elena Rybakina going into 2027. The players did not shake hands after the match, Al Jazeera noted; Ukrainian players have declined to shake hands with Russian and Belarusian opponents since Russia’s 2022 full-scale invasion, in line with their federation’s policy. Andreeva also won a custom purple Mercedes — though, at 19, she said she still needs a driver’s licence.
