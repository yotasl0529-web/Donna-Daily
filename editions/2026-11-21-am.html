NATO calls Russian missiles that came down in Poland a “grave violation”; Warsaw says it looks like a deliberate provocation

Official statementClaimPBS News, Al Jazeera and 1 more · Nov 20–21 (Kyiv bridge strikes covered Nov 20)

Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone fell in south-eastern Poland during Russia’s huge overnight attack on Ukraine on Nov. 20, Polish officials said. No one was hurt.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisRussian weapons have strayed into NATO territory before, but this time they came down near an exercise for a postwar force in Ukraine, and Poland’s leaders are openly calling it deliberate. That makes it harder to shrug off just as U.S. envoys push peace talks. The real test is what NATO does next: Article 5, the collective-defence clause, is a political decision, and Poland also has the option of Article 4, which only triggers consultations.
Full story, sources & checks3 sources

Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone fell in south-eastern Poland during Russia’s huge overnight attack on Ukraine on Nov. 20, Polish officials said. No one was hurt. Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said one object, a Kh-101 cruise missile, came down at Nowy Lipowiec about 50 km from the Ukrainian border; Al Jazeera reported the others fell at Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared to be “an intentional provocation”, timed to a two-week European military exercise in Poland preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it a “grave violation” of Polish airspace, and AP (via PBS) reported he was expected to visit Poland this weekend. On Nov. 21 Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski told the BBC the missiles had guidance systems, so it was “very unlikely” they had been knocked off course by Ukrainian electronic warfare. Russia had not commented, the BBC said. Separately, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told CNN that Vladimir Putin is “a killer”, after Donald Trump called the Russian leader his “friend” at the Shenzhen summit; Trump said on Nov. 20 that he “can’t speak” to whether Putin is a friend to the U.S.

Note: Accounts of distance differ: AP (via PBS) and Al Jazeera say one missile came down about 35 km from the exercise area; the BBC quotes Polish authorities as saying about 100 km. Both are given here. Russia’s intent is unconfirmed; “provocation” is the Polish government’s assessment.

Anthropic mathematician says he used Claude to prove the Riemann hypothesis; experts say checking it will take a long time

ClaimBBC News, DW · Nov 20–21

Levent Alpöge says an unreleased version of Claude helped him produce a proof of maths’ most famous unsolved problem, more than 250,000 pages long.

More2 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisIf the proof holds, it would be one of the biggest results in the history of mathematics and the strongest evidence yet that AI can do original research at the very top level. Until other mathematicians check it, though, it is a claim, and one that is unusually hard to check. Its timing, right after Anthropic’s IPO, means people will also scrutinise the motives behind it.

It has not been verified. Levent Alpöge, a mathematician on Anthropic’s technical staff, said on Friday, Nov. 20, that over about six weeks he used a non-public version of the company’s Claude chatbot to produce a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, the BBC and DW reported. The 1859 conjecture about how prime numbers are distributed is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million prize. Alpöge wrote that the work generated more than 250,000 pages, written in the proof-checking language Lean, and that he had not yet mastered all the mathematics involved. He also acknowledged the announcement came a day after Anthropic’s stock-market debut, calling the timing “horrible” and rejecting a cynical reading. Mathematician Terence Tao said verification is still some way off. Clay Institute president Martin Bridson, quoted by AP via DW, called it “an announcement of historic significance” but said “the journey… to a place of real understanding will be long and difficult.”

Note: The proof is unverified. “Solved” in some headlines goes further than the sources: the BBC says the claim would be significant “if found to be correct”.

Hong Kong proposes fines of up to HK$10 million and five years’ jail for building works that cause death or injury, after the Wang Fuk Court fire

Official proposalOfficial statementRTHK · Nov 20 (Wang Fuk Court fire anniversary is Nov 26)

The plans would require permits for large-scale maintenance projects.

More2 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisThe fire showed how loosely big repair jobs were supervised in a city full of ageing high-rises. The proposed penalties are much stiffer, but they won’t take effect until mid-2027. The government’s comments on memorials, allowing them while warning about “hidden agendas”, show how politically sensitive the anniversary still is.

Separately, the chief secretary said lawful memorials for the fire’s 168 victims are “absolutely permitted”. Hong Kong’s Development Bureau has proposed tightening the Buildings Ordinance after last November’s Wang Fuk Court fire in Tai Po, RTHK reported on Nov. 20. Large-scale maintenance works, on buildings of four storeys or more with scaffolding covering a whole side across at least four floors, would no longer count as minor works: they would need a Buildings Department permit, prescribed plans and a supervision plan. Works that cause or may cause death, injury or property damage would become a new offence with maximum penalties of HK$10 million and five years in prison, up from HK$1 million and three years. Using substandard materials could bring HK$3 million and three years. Contractors found to have engaged in bribery or bid-rigging could be struck off registers for three years without a disciplinary hearing. The government aims to table the bill in January and bring it into force by mid-2027. Ahead of the Nov. 26 anniversary of the fire, which killed 168 people, Chief Secretary Eric Chan said lawful memorial events are “absolutely permitted”, while warning people to beware of those with “ulterior motives”.

NPR · Nov 20 (on display through Sunday, Nov. 22)

The only known draft of Martin Luther King Jr.’s 1963 March on Washington speech, handwritten in blue ink on yellow legal paper, is on public display for the first time at the National Center for Civil and Human Rights in Atlanta, NPR reported on Nov. 20. According to the centre’s CEO, Jill Savitt, King originally titled it “Normalcy Never Again”, and the draft doesn’t contain the famous “I have a dream” lines, which he improvised on the day. NPR said the document will be on exhibit through Sunday.
