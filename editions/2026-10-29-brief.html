United States

Deep dive: Midterms in 5 days

What's happening
Election Day is Tue, Nov 3. In the final PBS/NPR/Marist poll (Oct 19–20), likely voters prefer Democrats for Congress 52% to 44%, and Trump's job approval is 40% approve, 59% disapprove.
Background
More than 30 million ballots had already been cast, per the University of Florida Election Lab (as cited by PBS). Voters rank cost of living (34%) and preserving democracy (33%) as their top issues.
Key players
Trump rallied in Ohio on Wednesday while Obama campaigned in Atlanta for Keisha Lance Bottoms and Jon Ossoff (AP). The Justice Department sued to block Alaska from certifying its Senate race if Dan Sullivan loses to Mary Peltola, and Trump called for prosecuting Peltola over a same-name candidate.
What's next
Strategists from both parties told PBS the House leans Democratic and the Senate is closer; a Republican strategist expects the GOP to hold the Senate by a seat or two. Half of Republican likely voters plan to vote in person on Election Day, so early counts may lean Democratic and then shift.
Why it matters to you
Control of Congress shapes tariffs, the Iran war and student costs. In the poll, voters under 45 favour Democrats by 22 points but are the least enthusiastic about voting. If you're registered in NY, you vote Tuesday.
Source check
PBS/Marist numbers were read in full. AP's coverage matches on the rallies and the Alaska fight. Forecast models vary (I saw 51–60% Democratic Senate odds), so treat any single number loosely.

Deep dive: Fed holds, and a hike may come in December

What happened
On Oct 28 the Fed held its key rate at 3.75–4% in a 10–2 vote. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented because they wanted a quarter-point hike. The statement says inflation "remains elevated".
Background
The Fed hiked in September for the first time in three years, against Trump's calls for cuts (AP). Chair Kevin Warsh said energy prices have risen again and that inflation has run above target for close to six years.
What's next
Reuters quoted an investment chief expecting a December hike. CNBC reports the Fed will hold fewer policy meetings from 2027, and one 2027 meeting will cover strategy only, with no rate vote.
Why it matters to you
Credit card, car loan and private student loan rates stay high or could rise further.
Source check
The Fed statement and Warsh transcript are primary sources; CNBC, AP and Reuters agree.

Deep dive: Iran, a letter before possible strikes

What happened
VP JD Vance wrote to Mohsen Rezaei, Iran's national security chief, saying any talks to head off US strikes "require discussions on the nuclear issue" (Axios; first reported by the WSJ). Iranian state news says Tehran received the letter but has not replied (Jerusalem Post).
Background
The US is at war with Iran and enforcing a naval blockade. On Oct 8, Trump said there would be no new strikes before the midterms. Xinhua, citing US media, reports the Pentagon's cost estimate is now at least $47B.
Framing gap
Axios says Trump is weighing a resumption of major combat after the election. Trump publicly described the letter as being about getting medical supplies into Iran.
Sticking point
Iran wants the blockade lifted and its funds freed first; the US wants nuclear talks before any relief (Axios).
Why it matters to you
The war is a main driver of the gas and energy inflation that both the Fed and the Bank of Canada cite. Renewed strikes after Nov 3 could push prices up again.

Canada

Deep dive: First rate hike in three years

What happened
On Oct 28 the Bank of Canada raised its rate a quarter point to 2.5%, its first hike since July 2023. Governor Tiff Macklem said more hikes are possible but there is "no predetermined path" (Reuters).
Background
September inflation was 3.2%, driven almost entirely by energy. The bank now sees inflation ending 2026 at 3.5% and getting back near 2% only by mid-2028.
Immediate effect
All five big banks raised prime to 4.70%, so variable-rate mortgages cost more right away (CBC).
Where sources differ
CIBC thinks the rate won't need to go above 2.75%. The next decision is Dec 9.
Why it matters to you
The loonie firmed only slightly, to C$1.4050 per US$1, so your US dollars still go far in Toronto.

Deep dive: Canada–US trade war and new tariffs on Oct 30

What happened
Carney said Oct 28 that Canada is "not in a position right now" to sign a deal with the US and will agree only "when it suits Canadians" (CBC). Trump said at a rally that Canada is "willing to give us anything".
Background
Talks collapsed in August. The US imposed Section 338 tariffs, and Canada answered with dollar-for-dollar levies.
What changes Oct 30
A White House proclamation dated Oct 23 swaps which Canadian goods are tariffed: it removes plywood, some paper and refrigeration equipment, and adds valves, carpets and others. The tariffs apply even to USMCA-qualifying goods.
Framing gap
The White House says Canada "walked away from a near-final trade deal". Ottawa says the August offer crossed its red lines. CBC sources say a deal before Nov 3 is not imminent.
Why it matters to you
There is no effect on travellers, but some prices for goods crossing the border could change.

Hong Kong

Deep dive: Wang Fuk Court fire report, apology and resignation

What happened
On Oct 29, Chief Executive John Lee released the independent committee's report, apologised and bowed with top officials. Housing Secretary Winnie Ho is stepping down to take political responsibility, and Deputy Financial Secretary Michael Wong becomes acting housing chief.
Background
On Nov 26, 2025, fire engulfed seven blocks at the Wang Fuk Court estate in Tai Po during renovation work, killing 168 people and displacing more than 4,600. Nearly 40% of residents were 65 or older (BBC).
What the report found
The fire was most likely started by a cigarette, and non-fire-retardant scaffolding nets and foam boards spread it (BBC). The contractor and consultant are named the "main culprits". Four government departments had "systemic and pervasive" failings (HKFP). Bid-rigging had triad links.
What's next
There will be 7 reform areas and 120 recommendations. Bid-rigging becomes a criminal offence under the organised crime law, and a police–ICAC–Competition Commission task force will be set up (RTHK). A civil-service accountability probe reports within 3 months.
Where coverage differs
HKFP highlights reporters asking why the labour, security and development secretaries kept their jobs. Lee said it was his decision. The government release focuses on reforms.
Why it matters to you
Many Hong Kong families live in ageing high-rises like Wang Fuk. This is the city's biggest accountability moment in years.

China

Deep dive: Party plenum sets the 2027 congress

What happened
The Communist Party's Central Committee met Oct 26–29 in Beijing and adopted a resolution on "full and rigorous Party self-governance" (discipline). It set the 21st Party Congress for the second half of 2027.
Official framing (Xinhua)
It "fully affirmed" the Politburo's work and called on the party to rally around Xi "at its core".
Independent framing (Reuters)
279 of the 376 members named in 2022 attended, the highest absence rate since Mao. 33 officials were expelled, including former Politburo members Ma Xingrui and Zhang Youxia. General Zhang Shengmin was unexpectedly elevated to the Politburo.
What's next
The 2027 congress is the twice-a-decade leadership reshuffle, and Xi is widely expected to take a fourth term (Reuters).
Why it matters to you
It shows Xi's grip, including over the military, ahead of 2027, which shapes US–China and Taiwan tensions.

Global

Deep dive: Spain's high-speed train crash

What happened
On Oct 28 near Toledo, a Renfe high-speed train carrying 397 people hit a stopped Iryo train from behind. It was authorised to restart at normal speed after the LZB safety system failed on a 63 km stretch, and reached up to 200 km/h (Reuters, Euronews).
Death toll
Sources differ: 68 (Euronews, AP, Xinhua) versus at least 69 (Reuters' updated graphic, the Guardian).
Key players
Transport Minister Óscar Puente called the control-centre order "inexplicable" and says the drivers are not to blame. Unions say the leaked audio was edited. One expert told Reuters the LZB system was outdated and was meant to be replaced in 2021.
What's next
There are three days of national mourning, the Civil Guard and accident investigators are reviewing the black boxes, and Madrid–Andalusia high-speed service is suspended. It is Spain's second deadly crash on the line this year, after 46 died in January.

Deep dive: Israel's election deadlock

Result
With the near-final count, Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats, the Zionist opposition 48 and Arab-led parties a record 19. Forming a government takes 61 of 120 seats. Likud is the largest party with 27 (JFeed, Jerusalem Post).
Framing gap
Anti-Netanyahu parties total 67 seats if you count the Arab parties (Xinhua, Guardian). The Jewish-led opposition mostly refuses to govern with them, so that 67 is only a number on paper (Times of Israel).
Key players
Netanyahu told his far-right partners "we are staying together". Gadi Eisenkot rejects serving under Netanyahu. Likud alleged Arab-party election fraud (Jerusalem Post).
What's next
The president consults the parties and picks who tries to form a government. A repeat election is possible.
