Polls open across the U.S. as voters decide control of Congress on midterm Election Day

ReportedUS · Politics & current affairsNov 3, 2026 (Election Day morning)

Election Day voting is under way after more than 57 million Americans cast early ballots. All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are on the ballot; Democrats need to flip a handful of seats in each chamber to take control from Republicans.

Why it matters
Control of Congress will shape how much of President Trump’s agenda can advance in the final two years of his term — and whether Democrats can block or investigate it.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

Election Day voting is under way after more than 57 million Americans cast early ballots, according to the University of Florida Voting Lab as reported by CBS News. All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are on the ballot, and 36 states are electing governors. Democrats need to flip three House seats and four Senate seats to wrest control from Republicans, per AP and CBS. The first polling stations opened in Vermont at 5 a.m. ET, BBC reported, with East Coast states including New York, New Jersey, Virginia and Maine opening at 6 a.m. NPR said more than 54 million people had already voted by early Tuesday and that Democrats are favored to win the House while the Senate remains anyone’s guess. Results are scheduled to start arriving after polls close this evening; many close races may take days to call. The Supreme Court on Monday denied the Trump administration’s emergency request to make California set aside about 20,000 disputed mail ballots — covered in Monday’s Evening Edition.

Note: No results are in as of this morning edition. Early-vote totals move through the day; BBC cited about 58.7 million as of late Monday night from the same Florida lab CBS used for the 57 million-plus figure.

Justice Department deploys about 1,000 election monitors to all 50 states for the first time

In a Tuesday morning release, the Justice Department said it is mounting its largest Election Day monitoring effort ever — about 1,000 monitors and observers, for the first time covering every state — to check compliance with federal voting-rights laws.

Why it matters
A first-time nationwide DOJ presence on Election Day is a major administrative move on a day when trust in election administration is itself a campaign issue.
Read more· full story & 2 sources

In a Tuesday morning release, the Justice Department said it is mounting its largest Election Day monitoring effort ever — about 1,000 monitors and observers, for the first time covering every state. The Civil Rights Division is coordinating the deployment, which includes Division staff, other DOJ components, U.S. Attorney’s Offices and federal observers from the Office of Personnel Management. Assistant Attorney General Harmeet K. Dhillon said the department is “deploying the largest monitoring effort in DOJ history” and pursuing “transparency for elections in an assertive and nondiscriminatory manner.” Monitors are to check compliance with statutes including the Voting Rights Act, National Voter Registration Act, Help America Vote Act, Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act, Civil Rights Acts and the ADA; the release lists jurisdictions in all 50 states. CBS News also reported the 50-state plan early Tuesday.

Note: The release describes observation and complaint channels; it does not say monitors will run elections or change local procedures.

Flooding and storms force polling-place moves in Florida and elsewhere on Election Day

At least 18 polling locations in seven Florida counties were relocated after flash flooding, NBC News reported. A tornado damaged an Oklahoma polling site, and flood-hit counties in Utah and Colorado were given special ballot options.

Why it matters
Even limited polling-place moves can confuse voters in competitive states — and become fodder for election-integrity arguments online.
Read more· full story & 2 sources

At least 18 polling locations across seven Florida counties — Brevard, Highlands, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie — were moved after intense rainfall caused flash floods Monday into Tuesday morning, NBC News reported. Some coastal areas saw as much as 22 inches of rain in 24 hours; Gov. Ron DeSantis declared a state of emergency in the seven counties. BBC, citing its partner CBS, separately said Florida election officials were temporarily relocating 19 polling stations under that order. In Oklahoma, Cimarron County moved a polling place after a Sunday tornado damaged the Keyes Community Center. Utah’s lieutenant governor and Colorado’s secretary of state authorized special electronic-ballot options for flood-isolated voters in San Juan County, Utah, and La Plata and Archuleta counties, Colorado. Weather’s effect on turnout is being watched, though researchers note early and mail voting have softened rain’s historical impact.

Note: BBC’s “19” and NBC’s “at least 18” are close but not identical counts of Florida moves; both attribute the authority to DeSantis’s emergency order.

B.C. First Nations go to court over Pacific Link’s ‘national interest’ designation

Several First Nations, including the St’át’imc Chiefs Council, filed federal judicial reviews seeking to overturn Ottawa’s decision to list the Alberta-to-B.C. oil pipeline as a project of national interest under the Building Canada Act.

Why it matters
A court fight over consultation could slow one of the Carney government’s marquee energy projects.
Read more· full story & 1 source

Several First Nations, including the St’át’imc Chiefs Council representing 11 communities, filed federal judicial reviews seeking to overturn Ottawa’s decision to list the Pacific Link oil pipeline as a project of national interest under the Building Canada Act, CBC News reported Monday. The proposed line would move about one million barrels a day from Bruderheim, Alta., to a deepwater port near Delta, B.C. Listing shifts the federal focus from whether to build to how, by fast-tracking approvals. The nations argue the listing “effectively acts as an approval” and that duty-to-consult obligations must be met before a project is listed. Chief Bonnie Adolph said her community was refused consultation because its territory is not directly on the route, despite Fraser River watershed concerns. Other applicants include Boston Bar, Cheam, Lower Nicola, Kwantlen, Kwikwetlem, Skeetchestn and Tsartlip, plus Cold Lake First Nations in Alberta. Pacific Link said Indigenous participation, including possible equity ownership, is central to the project. CBC said it had sought a federal response.

Note: The nations’ claim that Canada breached its duty to consult is their court argument, not a finding.

China readies a third market-support swap and makes buyback lending permanent

The PBOC said it would run another Securities, Funds and Insurance companies Swap Facility operation on Tuesday, with applications topping 100 billion yuan. Share-buyback relending becomes a long-term tool and its rate falls to 1%.

Why it matters
Beijing is leaning on liquidity tools again as it tries to stabilise equities without a dramatic policy U-turn.
Read more· full story & 3 sources

The PBOC said it would run a third Securities, Funds and Insurance companies Swap Facility (SFISF) operation on Tuesday, with a one-year term that can be extended; 31 securities and fund firms applied for more than 100 billion yuan (about $14.9 billion), Xinhua reported. After a meeting with the financial regulator and CSRC, the central bank said it would expand participants, lower swap fees and refine collateral rules. The share-buyback and stake-increase relending facility, launched as a temporary 2024 tool, becomes a long-term arrangement and its relending rate drops from 1.25% to 1%. Xinhua said signed buyback-related loans total about 440 billion yuan, with more than 280 billion disbursed. Caixin also reported the package as support for a declining stock market.

Cyberattacks hit supermarket fridges across nine European countries

Remote tampering switched refrigeration into defrost mode, spoiling food from Belgium to Sweden. Belgian federal prosecutors opened a criminal investigation; the EU agriculture commissioner called it a deliberate threat to the food supply chain.

Why it matters
A cross-border hit on cold chains shows how everyday connected equipment can become critical infrastructure overnight.
Read more· full story & 3 sources

Suspected cyberattacks on Nov 2 hit grocery refrigeration systems in at least nine countries — Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal and Sweden — Euronews reported, forcing some stores to discard food after temperatures rose overnight. Belgium was among the hardest hit, with dozens of Delhaize, Carrefour, Intermarché and Spar outlets affected; federal prosecutors opened a criminal probe, VRT confirmed. Attackers reportedly switched evaporators to defrost mode, a pattern refrigeration firms linked to an October attack on French chains. Danish supplier Danfoss said outdated software left some customers exposed. EU Agriculture Commissioner Christophe Hansen said the incidents were “a deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain.” No one has claimed responsibility.

Kimi Antonelli becomes Formula 1’s youngest world champion at the Mexico City GP

The 20-year-old Mercedes driver sealed the title with third place behind Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, breaking Sebastian Vettel’s age record and becoming Italy’s first champion since 1953.

Why it matters
A teenage-to-twenties title rewrite marks a changing of the guard in F1’s driver hierarchy.
Read more· full story & 2 sources

Kimi Antonelli sealed his first Formula 1 world title at 20 years and 68 days by finishing third at the Mexico City Grand Prix, BBC Sport and Reuters/Al Jazeera reported. Red Bull’s Max Verstappen won a red-flagged race and Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton was second after a tense first-lap fight with teammate Charles Leclerc. Antonelli’s championship lead became unassailable at 119 points; he broke Sebastian Vettel’s record as youngest champion by more than three years and is the first Italian title winner since Alberto Ascari in 1953. The race included two safety cars and a long stoppage after Valtteri Bottas crashed. Mercedes leads the constructors’ standings heading to Brazil.
