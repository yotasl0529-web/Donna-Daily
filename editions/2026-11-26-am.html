U.S. proposes new tariffs on 14 economies, including China, the EU and Japan; Beijing says it “reserves the right” to respond

The U.S. Trade Representative on Wednesday, Nov. 25, proposed new tariffs on goods from 14 economies it says fuel “structural excess capacity”, with proposed ceilings ranging from 15% to 35% depending on the country. The duties are proposals: public comments are due Jan. 5 and hearings start Jan. 25, 2027.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisTariffs are paid by importers and often passed on in prices, so if these go ahead in 2027 they could show up in the cost of electronics, clothes and other everyday goods. For now it is a proposal with a comment period, which is why trading partners are reacting with words rather than retaliation. The China rate matters most: the current U.S.–China truce runs to Jan. 10, just before the hearings.

The U.S. Trade Representative on Wednesday, Nov. 25, proposed new tariffs on goods from 14 economies it says fuel “structural excess capacity”, with proposed ceilings ranging from 15% to 35% depending on the country. The duties are proposals: public comments are due Jan. 5 and hearings start Jan. 25, 2027. The 14 are China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, South Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India; the USTR made no finding on Mexico or Norway. According to the USTR’s release, the proposed ceilings include up to 15% for the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan, up to 18% for several Southeast Asian countries and Bangladesh, up to 20% for most Chinese goods and Singapore, up to 25% for India and up to 35% for Vietnam, in each case adjusted for existing deals and duties. Reports differ on the effective rate for China: the USTR release lists up to 20% for most goods, AFP says the rate could double to 40%, and the Guardian says stacking on other duties could take some goods to 45%. China’s commerce ministry said on Thursday that “overcapacity” was a “pretext for protectionism” and that China “reserves the right to take necessary measures”; Japan called the move “regrettable” and Singapore said it was “very disappointed”. Brazil, which faces a separate proposal, said it “will not yield to this type of threat”. The USTR’s Jamieson Greer said the plan would “level the playing field for American workers”.

Note: Follows the Nov 25 edition, which reported the BBC’s account that tariffs were being prepared; the USTR has now published the proposals. Reactions from China, Japan and Singapore are from RTHK and France 24 (AFP/Reuters copy).

The Supreme Court of Canada says it will release its decision on Quebec’s secularism law, Bill 21, at 9:45 a.m. ET on Friday, Nov. 27, CBC reports.
