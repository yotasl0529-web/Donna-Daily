Democrats hold both chambers as the count goes on in Alaska, Mississippi and about 60 House races

NPR, AP News and 2 more · Nov 4

A day after AP race calls gave Democrats control of the House and the Senate, two Senate races and roughly 60 House seats were still uncalled on Wednesday. The open races will set the size of the new majorities, not which party leads.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisEvery extra seat changes how much room Democratic leaders have to pass bills, launch investigations and override, or fail to override, presidential vetoes when the new Congress meets in January.
Full story, sources & checks5 sources

A day after AP race calls gave Democrats control of the House and the Senate, two Senate races and roughly 60 House seats were still uncalled on Wednesday. The open races will set the size of the new majorities, not which party leads. NPR, using AP race calls, reported that Democrats flipped seven Republican-held Senate seats (North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and Maine), returning them to full control of Congress for the first time since 2023. AP said that as of 4 p.m. ET Wednesday only a handful of Senate and governor races were uncalled and that 59 House races remained uncalled. El Cronista, citing AP tallies from Wednesday morning, put the Senate at 54 Democrats to 44 Republicans with Alaska and Mississippi still open. Slate reported on Wednesday evening that Democrats had officially won 237 House seats, with several Arizona, Florida, North Carolina and Colorado races among those still counting. California alone had 11 uncalled House seats, and its counties have until Dec. 3 to finish counting, El Cronista reported. Race-by-race results are in our Midterms 2026 special edition, listed under Past editions.

Note: Seat totals move as ballots are counted; figures here are as of the times the linked outlets published them on Wednesday. Donna Daily does not call any race.

More2 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisA fast-rising yuan squeezes Chinese exporters; the move signals Beijing wants the currency to stay broadly stable.

The People’s Bank of China said it will raise the share of foreign-currency deposits banks must hold with it from 4% to 6% from Nov. 15, Xinhua reported, a step meant to ease upward pressure on the yuan. It is the first increase in nearly five years. The yuan gained 4.2% against the dollar in the first nine months of the year, and the onshore rate strengthened for 14 straight trading days from Oct. 14, according to Xinhua. Raising the ratio drains dollar liquidity at banks, which tends to cool the currency. The South China Morning Post also reported the move as aimed at easing appreciation pressure.
