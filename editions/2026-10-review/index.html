Alberta votes three to one to stay in Canada, but backs tighter rules on newcomers

About 75% of voters rejected starting a legal process toward a binding separation referendum. The same ballot narrowly approved five immigration questions and rejected four constitutional ones.

Official figuresReportedVote Oct 19; official results Oct 29
Why it matters
The result settles the separation question in Alberta for now, but the immigration votes give Premier Danielle Smith a mandate she can press against Ottawa.
Read more· full story & 3 sources

Elections Alberta’s official count gave the “remain a province in Canada” option 74.95% (1,556,316 votes) against 25.05% for asking the government to begin the constitutional process for a binding separation vote. Turnout on that question was 70.4%, about 10 points above the 2023 provincial election. Questions on limiting immigration and restricting newcomers’ access to provincial programs passed with 52–58% support, while proposals to change how judges are chosen and to abolish the Senate failed. Premier Danielle Smith had urged a vote to remain while backing the other nine questions, according to the Globe.

Note: The Globe’s early-results story ran on Oct 20; the official certified figures came from Elections Alberta on Oct 29.

Judge orders federal officials to stop threatening election workers as DOJ presses ballot fights

A federal judge in California temporarily barred the Justice and Homeland Security departments from threatening or coercing state and local election officials. The administration is appealing.

ReportedOfficial statementOrder Oct 30; hearing set for Nov 13
Why it matters
Elections in the U.S. are run by states. Whether federal agencies can pressure local officials during the count is a test of that system in the final days before the Nov 3 midterms.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

Judge Trina Thompson’s temporary restraining order covers demands to set aside ballots, purge voters or stop processing them over alleged ineligibility, including threats of prosecution, and requires the Justice Department to report related subpoenas or search-warrant requests. It followed weeks in which the department sought to separate ballots from alleged noncitizens in California and Washington and asked Alaska to segregate ballots in its Senate race; an appeals court twice refused its California request. The order runs to Nov 16, with a hearing on a longer injunction on Nov 13.

Note: The order’s terms are summarised from the court filing as described in a search excerpt and in news reports; the administration disputes that it intimidated anyone and has appealed.

News · Canada

Parti Québécois returns to power with a minority as the CAQ is wiped out

Paul St-Pierre Plamondon’s sovereigntists won about 60 of 127 seats on less than 30% of the vote, short of the 64 needed for a majority. The governing CAQ lost every seat, and Christine Fréchette quit as leader the next day.

ReportedElection Oct 5; Fréchette resigned Oct 6
Why it matters
A sovereigntist government in Quebec reopens the national-unity file just as Ottawa is managing a trade war with the U.S., though a minority makes a referendum unlikely soon.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

The PQ grew from three seats in 2022 to roughly 60, while the Coalition Avenir Québec, which held 79 seats before the campaign, won none. The Liberals became the official opposition and the Conservatives won seats in the National Assembly for the first time in decades. St-Pierre Plamondon promised a third independence referendum but said it would wait until after Donald Trump leaves office, around 2029–30; as a minority premier he would need Québec Solidaire’s support to hold one.

Note: Seat counts were still being finalised on election night (CBC reported 59, later counts 60); the popular-vote share is about 28% per the Globe and “less than 30 per cent” per CBC.
