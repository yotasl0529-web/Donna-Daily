Supreme Court turns down Trump administration's bid to make California set aside about 20,000 disputed mail ballots

ReportedAllegationUS · Politics & current affairsNov 2, 2026 (evening)

On the eve of the midterms, the Supreme Court denied the Justice Department's emergency request to make California keep separate the mail ballots of more than 20,000 people the administration says are likely noncitizens. Lower courts had already refused.

Why it matters
California has more than 23 million registered voters and had received over 6 million mail ballots by Sunday, CBS News reported. The ruling means ballots are processed under the state's normal rules while the underlying lawsuit continues.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

On the eve of the midterms, the Supreme Court denied the Justice Department's emergency request to make California keep separate the mail ballots of more than 20,000 people the administration says are likely noncitizens. Lower courts had already refused. The department had sent California's secretary of state and Los Angeles County's top election official a list of about 20,000 registered voters it says are not citizens, and asked that their ballots be held back until citizenship could be verified. State and county officials questioned the list's accuracy and warned that eligible voters could be disenfranchised. On Oct 28, U.S. District Judge David Carter refused a temporary order, writing that voters must not lose a core right 'because of unfounded allegations stemming from a procedure rife with uncertainty and errors'. A divided 9th Circuit panel (2-1) also refused on Friday, Oct 30. Solicitor General D. John Sauer then asked the justices on Monday morning to order the ballots kept apart, arguing that once they are mixed in 'there is no way to get the challenged ballots back'. NPR and AP reported Monday evening that the court had denied the request.

Note: As of our check, the Supreme Court's online docket listed only the application (filed Nov 2), not the order, and we had not seen the order's text, so we can't say whether any justices dissented. The noncitizen accusation is the administration's and has not been proven in court.

Why it matters
Leaked booking details make scam calls sound convincing. Anyone who has flown or shopped with these firms should be wary of 'refund' calls.
Read more· full story & 2 sources

Hong Kong's Privacy Commissioner has opened formal investigations into data-breach reports from airlines and electronics retailers, including Cathay Pacific, Hong Kong Airlines, Malaysia Airlines, YOHO and Broadway. It has received 51 complaints and 48 enquiries, with individual losses from about HK$10,000 to more than HK$1.5 million. The watchdog warned of 'precision scams', where fraudsters use leaked flight or purchase details to pose as airline or shop staff offering refunds, returns or compensation. Real merchants won't ask for deposits, handling fees or transfers to 'verify' an account, it said; people should check through official websites, hotlines or stores before paying or sharing card details.
