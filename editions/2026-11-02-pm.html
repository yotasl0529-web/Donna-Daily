AllegationNPR, AP News and 2 more · Nov 2 (evening)

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisCalifornia has more than 23 million registered voters and had received over 6 million mail ballots by Sunday, CBS News reported. The ruling means ballots are processed under the state's normal rules while the underlying lawsuit continues.
Full story, sources & checks4 sources

On the eve of the midterms, the Supreme Court denied the Justice Department's emergency request to make California keep separate the mail ballots of more than 20,000 people the administration says are likely noncitizens. Lower courts had already refused. The department had sent California's secretary of state and Los Angeles County's top election official a list of about 20,000 registered voters it says are not citizens, and asked that their ballots be held back until citizenship could be verified. State and county officials questioned the list's accuracy and warned that eligible voters could be disenfranchised. On Oct 28, U.S. District Judge David Carter refused a temporary order, writing that voters must not lose a core right 'because of unfounded allegations stemming from a procedure rife with uncertainty and errors'. A divided 9th Circuit panel (2-1) also refused on Friday, Oct 30. Solicitor General D. John Sauer then asked the justices on Monday morning to order the ballots kept apart, arguing that once they are mixed in 'there is no way to get the challenged ballots back'. NPR and AP reported Monday evening that the court had denied the request.

Note: As of our check, the Supreme Court's online docket listed only the application (filed Nov 2), not the order, and we had not seen the order's text, so we can't say whether any justices dissented. The noncitizen accusation is the administration's and has not been proven in court.

Official statement A government, official body or company's own statement, figures or proposal, attributed to it.

Claim An assertion by a person, government or group that hasn't been independently confirmed.

Allegation Claimed, charged or argued in court; not proven.

Analysis Interpretation or opinion — the outlet's, an expert's, or Donna Daily's own — marked as such.

Unnamed sources Rests partly on sources the outlet did not name.

State media Relies on state-controlled media (e.g. Xinhua, CCTV, Russian state media).

Labels describe the type of claim a story rests on; they are not a verification grade. Story previews show a label only when it changes how to read the story (an official statement, claim, allegation, analysis, unnamed sources or state media); plain reporting is the default and is stated in each story’s expanded view. Older editions used “Fact”; it is shown as “Reported”. Why it matters is Donna Daily’s own analysis, kept apart from the sourced reporting. Photos are openly licensed (Wikimedia Commons CC0/public domain/CC BY/CC BY-SA, or US-government public domain), cropped to fit and credited beside each picture. “File photo” (with the year when known) means the picture shows the person or place, not the event in the story.
