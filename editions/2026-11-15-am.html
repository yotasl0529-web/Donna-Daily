Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisA rocket whose two stages can both be flown again is what would make Starship cheap enough for moon landings, huge satellite launches and Musk’s Mars plans. The Super Heavy booster, which SpaceX had also hoped to catch, aborted and exploded over the Gulf of Mexico on Friday. The 124-metre (407-foot) rocket lifted off from Starbase on Friday afternoon on its 15th full-scale test flight, the first in which SpaceX tried to recover both stages. Minutes after launch the booster triggered an automated abort and diverted to the ocean, where it exploded; Elon Musk said it had been “slightly low on fuel,” the BBC reported. The ship went on to deploy 26 Starlink satellites, circled Earth 14 times and then flew back to the tower, where the “chopsticks” arms caught it. SpaceX said the spacecraft, including its heat shield, appeared to work well, according to NPR. NASA, which is paying SpaceX to use Starship to land Artemis astronauts on the moon, welcomed the result. SpaceX had already caught boosters three times on earlier flights.
