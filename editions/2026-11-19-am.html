Trump, Xi and Putin hold the first three-way meeting of their countries’ leaders in Shenzhen

Official statementState mediaCBC News, Al Jazeera and 3 more · Nov 19 (previewed in the Nov 18 edition)

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met behind closed doors at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday, Nov. 19, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, the first such meeting of the three countries’ leaders.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisThe image of the three nuclear superpowers at one table was the main result so far, and that matters in itself: it signals that Washington is willing to deal with Beijing and Moscow together, which worries Kyiv, European capitals and smaller Asian countries that fear decisions being made over their heads. Watch for any follow-up on nuclear arms talks, which China has long refused to join, and for what each side later says was agreed on Ukraine and Iran.
Full story, sources & checks5 sources

Trump said they were “really making history” and called for talks on cutting nuclear arsenals; Putin said the three shared an interest in settling the war in Ukraine; Xi, who hosted, said peace among great powers keeps the world stable. Reports differ on how long the closed-door talks lasted: about 55 minutes (Al Jazeera), more than an hour (CBC) or an hour and 20 minutes (AFP via Hong Kong Free Press). No joint agreement had been reported when this edition was prepared, and NPR wrote that the leaders “offered few solutions toward peace.” Earlier in the day Trump met Putin, their first meeting since August 2025, and then Xi. Al Jazeera, citing Xinhua, reported Xi said nuclear weapons “should be comprehensively banned and thoroughly destroyed.” Japan’s Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, on her first trip to China since her Taiwan remarks strained ties a year ago, said she wants a “constructive and stable relationship” with Beijing; Japanese media cited by AFP said she did not get a one-to-one meeting with Xi.

UN top court finds Myanmar acted with “genocidal intent” against the Rohingya

Al Jazeera, The Guardian · Nov 19

The International Court of Justice in The Hague ruled on Thursday, Nov. 19, in the case brought by The Gambia, finding that Myanmar’s security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians in the 2017 military crackdown and acted with “genocidal intent” in specific cases, Al Jazeera reported.

More2 sources
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisThe ICJ has never before found a state responsible for genocide, so the reasoning on how to prove “intent” will matter far beyond Myanmar, including in the Gaza case. For Rohingya refugees, the ruling is a formal recognition of what they suffered, though the court has no police force to enforce it.

Court President Yuji Iwasawa said evidence showed some 10,000 Rohingya were killed; the court also said Myanmar violated its obligation to prevent genocide. Myanmar has said the operations were a response to attacks by Rohingya fighters. More than 730,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh, and about 1.2 million live in camps in Cox’s Bazar. The Guardian reported before the ruling that judges feared U.S. sanctions if the court found genocide, because the ruling could set a precedent for the court’s pending case over Israel’s actions in Gaza.
