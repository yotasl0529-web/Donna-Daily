Lead · Senate · National

Democrats win the House and the Senate in a sweeping rebuke of Trump

The Associated Press says Democrats won the Senate late Tuesday and the House early Wednesday. AP tallies at 9:10 AM ET: 54 Senate seats to 44, and 228 House seats to 139, with races still being counted.

ReportedCalled Nov 3–4 · seat counts as of 9:10 AM ET Wed
Why it matters
From Jan. 3, Democrats will control what Congress votes on, its investigations and, in the Senate, confirmations for Trump’s last two years. Trump keeps his veto.
Read more· full story & 5 sources

AP results carried by the Guardian at 9:10 AM ET showed Democrats (counting two independents who caucus with them) at 54 Senate seats to Republicans’ 44, a net gain of seven, with 33 of 35 races called. Alaska and Mississippi were still uncalled. In the House, Democrats had 228 seats to 139, a net gain of 25, with 367 of 435 races called; 218 is a majority. AP said it called Senate control after a flurry of Democratic wins late Tuesday and the House early Wednesday, far faster than 2022, when the Senate call took four days and the House eight. AP’s main story says voters’ frustration over high prices, the Iran war and government corruption drove the result, with Democrats flipping states Trump won by double digits two years ago. Ballotpedia’s own count at 1:30 AM put the floor at 54–44 in the Senate and 231–141 in the House; totals will keep moving as states count.

Note: Seat totals differ slightly between outlets because each uses its own race calls; this edition uses AP’s counts as published by the Guardian and says when it uses another desk.

Seven Senate flips, from Maine to Kansas to South Carolina

AP says Democrats took Republican-held seats in Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina. Four sitting Republican senators lost.

ReportedCalled Nov 3–4 (AP)
Why it matters
Democrats needed four pickups for a majority. Winning seven, several in states Trump carried easily in 2024, gives them room to spare.
Read more· full story & 3 sources

In North Carolina, former Gov. Roy Cooper beat Michael Whatley for an open seat, the first Democratic Senate win there since 2008, Ballotpedia notes; AP reported Cooper held a double-digit lead when the race was called. In Maine, Sen. Susan Collins conceded to Troy Jackson, who replaced Graham Platner as the Democratic nominee in July. In Ohio, Sherrod Brown beat Sen. Jon Husted in the special election for JD Vance’s old seat, a race AP says topped $500 million in spending. Josh Turek won Iowa’s open seat over Ashley Hinson; Adam Hamilton beat Sen. Roger Marshall in Kansas, which last elected a Democratic senator in 1932; and pediatrician Annie Andrews beat Sen. Darline Graham in South Carolina. Ballotpedia says a seven-seat loss puts 2026 among its “wave” elections. Texas is covered separately.

Texas turns blue at the top: Talarico wins the Senate race and Hinojosa ousts Abbott

Democrat James Talarico beat Attorney General Ken Paxton for the Senate, and Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott, who was seeking a record fourth term.

ReportedCalled Tue night, Nov 3
Why it matters
Texas has 40 electoral votes and had not elected a Democrat statewide in three decades; both parties will read this as a test case for 2028.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

The Guardian reports the pair delivered the party’s first statewide wins in Texas since 1994: the last Democratic senator elected there was Lloyd Bentsen in 1988 and the last Democratic governor was Ann Richards in 1990. Talarico becomes the youngest member of the Senate and Hinojosa the state’s first Latina governor. ABC’s results showed Talarico at about 55% to Paxton’s 43% with roughly 97% of the expected vote counted. Talarico told CBS Mornings that his win sent a message to Elon Musk, who CBS says spent more than $150 million backing Paxton: “Texas is not for sale.” Down the ballot, Republican and former Yankees star Mark Teixeira won the open 21st District, the Guardian reports.

Still uncalled: Alaska and Mississippi Senate races are on a knife-edge

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by about 600 votes in AP’s count. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by a few dozen votes with about 96% counted.

ReportedUncalled as of Wed morning
Why it matters
These two seats decide whether Democrats finish at 54, 55 or 56 seats, and either could end in legal fights; leads are not wins.
Read more· full story & 3 sources

AP figures shown by the Guardian on Wednesday morning had Peltola at 102,701 votes (48.2%) to Sullivan’s 102,075 (47.9%); neither race had been called. Alaska accepts overseas ballots for 15 days and does not run its ranked-choice tabulation until Nov. 18, which can swing results, the Alaska Current reports. The race was shadowed by a second candidate also named Daniel Sullivan; a Trump administration lawsuit seeking to block certification if the incumbent lost was dismissed by a federal judge on Sunday, CBS reports. In Mississippi, CBS rated the race a toss-up with only a few dozen votes separating the candidates. Neither result changes Senate control.

Note: Donna Daily does not call races. Both are listed as uncalled by AP and as toss-ups by CBS News.

Democrats pass 218 in the House, with 68 races still counting

AP says Democrats crossed the 218-seat majority line early Wednesday. Its tally at 9:10 AM ET: 228 Democrats, 139 Republicans and 68 races uncalled, many of them in slow-counting states such as California.

ReportedCalled early Wed, Nov 4 (AP)
Why it matters
The House majority brings the speaker’s gavel, every committee chair and subpoena power, so expect investigations of the administration from January.
Read more· full story & 5 sources

Ballotpedia counted 14 Republican House incumbents, and no Democrats, defeated as of 1:30 AM. Nine states redrew their maps mid-decade before this election, a push encouraged by Trump; Ballotpedia says the new lines should have helped Republicans by about nine seats, but if current leads hold Democrats will net about 15 seats across those states. AP’s live coverage says Democrats flipped districts Trump won by as much as 18 points in 2024. The Guardian’s live blog says the final Democratic total could reach 240 or more. Brooklyn’s Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become the first Black Speaker of the House.

Ballot measures: abortion rights win in Missouri, Virginia and Nevada; Missouri voters scrap the GOP map

Voters decided 145 ballot questions in more than three dozen states, AP reports. Abortion-rights supporters won in three states, and Missouri voters threw out a Republican-drawn congressional map.

ReportedResults as of Wed, Nov 4
Why it matters
Ballot measures go around legislatures. These results tell both parties where voters stand on abortion, election rules and redistricting.
Read more· full story & 4 sources

Missouri voters defeated an amendment that would have brought back most abortion restrictions and banned some medical procedures for transgender minors. Virginia added a “fundamental right to reproductive freedom” to its constitution, and Nevada gave final approval to an abortion-rights amendment. Idaho voters rejected an abortion-rights law. In Missouri, voters also rejected the mid-decade map designed to win Republicans a Kansas City-area seat; Maryland approved an amendment that could allow new districts in 2028. Florida approved a major property-tax break for homeowners. California voters rejected a stricter voter-ID amendment, AP reports, while Ballotpedia counts citizenship-to-vote amendments passing in Arkansas, Kansas, South Dakota and West Virginia, and voter-ID expansions in North Carolina and Ohio.

Note: Ballotpedia’s 1:30 AM roundup still listed Nevada and Idaho as uncalled; AP’s 4:21 AM story reports both results.

AP says it is too early to call Proposition 40, which would impose a one-time 5% tax on billionaires’ wealth.

By Victor Davis Hanson · The Free Press

Hanson, a conservative historian, argues the losses fit a long pattern of midterm defeats for the White House party and may not derail Trump’s final two years.

OpinionNov 4
Why read it
This is the strongest version of the argument that Tuesday was normal midterm gravity and not a verdict on Trumpism.
Read more· the argument & 1 source

Hanson notes that since 1900 the president’s party has lost House seats about 90% of the time and Senate seats about 75%, though he says Trump’s losses may rival Clinton’s in 1994 and Obama’s in 2010. Without a veto-proof Senate, he argues, Democrats will block judicial and cabinet nominees, but Trump will keep governing by executive order at home and acting largely as he likes abroad. His success, Hanson writes, will rest on the economy and on denuclearizing Iran, and quickly. Most of the column is behind a paywall; this summary covers the free portion.

“Red and blue America have united” against Trump

By Ben Davis · The Guardian

Davis, a political data analyst who worked on Bernie Sanders’s 2020 campaign, argues that 2026 is not about one group or region swinging. He sees a rejection of Trump by every segment of the electorate.

OpinionNov 4
Why read it
This data-focused argument is the opposite of Hanson’s “normal midterm” reading.
Read more· the argument & 1 source

Davis contrasts this year with recent elections that each had a single story, such as the 2018 suburban revolt or the 2022 backlash over abortion. He argues people tolerated Trump’s style in exchange for promised economic and physical security, and that his second term broke that bargain. Even the rosy polls, he says, missed the size of the wave. His conclusion: Trump has “depolarized” America, as truly red states like Texas, Kansas and South Carolina went blue.
