More1 source
Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisWith the 85th anniversary of Pearl Harbor three weeks away, only a handful of people who were there are still alive. Johnson’s matter-of-fact memories — of missing the attack entirely below deck — are a reminder of how ordinary most of the sailors were.

Freeman Johnson, the oldest of the few remaining survivors of the Dec. 7, 1941, attack on Pearl Harbor, died on Saturday at his home in Centerville, Massachusetts, his daughter told the Associated Press. He was 106. Johnson was working below deck on a boiler of the light cruiser USS St. Louis during the Japanese attack and told the AP in May that he neither saw nor heard it — “I was inside a steam drum.” He later helped commission the battleship USS Iowa and watched Japan’s surrender in Tokyo Bay from the Iowa’s mast in September 1945. After the war he worked in a machinist shop, a convenience store and delivering meals to seniors, retiring from that last job at 90. The list of known survivors had stood at about a dozen names after the death of Ira “Ike” Schab, 105, in December 2025.
