United States

Deep dive: Midterms in 2 days

What's happening
Election Day is Tue, Nov 3. NBC News’s final national poll of registered voters (Oct 28–30, ±3.1 pts) has Democrats preferred for Congress 52%–45%, with a 21-point lead among independents. Trump’s approval is 40% and disapproval a poll high of 60%.
What voters say
About three in four are dissatisfied with the economy; 64% want a change from Trump’s direction (50% ‘a great deal’). Cost of living ranks first, then government corruption, threats to democracy, and immigration.
Where it gets close
USA Today (Nov 1) cites RealClearPolitics averages with Democrats narrowly ahead in Senate races in Texas (Talarico over Paxton), Michigan (El-Sayed over Rogers), and Maine (Jackson over Collins), all within typical poll error. House control starts from a thin GOP edge.
Framing gap
NBC shows Democrats popular as a check on Trump, not as a beloved brand (only 29% view Democrats positively vs 38% for Republicans). The Guardian (Nov 1) frames a possible ‘blue wave’; poll averages differ by a few points, so treat any single number loosely.
Why it matters to you
If you’re registered in New York, you can still early-vote today or go Tuesday. Control of Congress shapes tariffs, energy costs tied to the Iran conflict, and student-related federal policy.

NBC poll page read in full Nov 1. USA Today live midterm updates and Guardian interactive cited for race-level color; averages can disagree.

Canada

Deep dive: Clock patchwork and Carney’s EU pitch

Clocks
Most of Ontario and the US fell back one hour early Sunday. British Columbia (permanent Pacific daylight time) did not, so much of B.C. is now one hour ahead of Washington state (CBC, Oct 31).
EU associate membership
CBC’s The National reported Carney confirmed Canada is working toward becoming the EU’s first ‘associate member,’ short of accession, with substance to be defined in coming months and a European Council visit framed for June. Summit statements still leave the label unsettled among the 27 members.
Consumer watch
CBC Marketplace (Nov 1) says Air Canada and WestJet have used safety-related maintenance claims to cancel flights and deny compensation, while still flying those planes on other routes; the transport minister said he is reluctant to instruct the independent CTA.
Why it matters to you
Time-zone quirks affect calls with the West Coast. Airline compensation fights matter if you fly Air Canada (including any Toronto travel). Midterm outcomes in the US will ripple into Canada–US trade talks.

Hong Kong

Deep dive: Building-maintenance reform after Wang Fuk

What happened
On Sunday, Justice Secretary Paul Lam said legislative amendments after the Wang Fuk Court fire aim at a ‘revolutionary change’ to the building-maintenance ecosystem, driving dishonest contractors out so honest firms will re-enter (RTHK).
What's next
Commerce Secretary Algernon Yau said criminalising bid-rigging would give stronger tools and hopes amendments can pass by April 2027. Bills are slated for LegCo within this year.
Also today
The Standard’s Nov 1 night recap: first Northern Metropolis Marathon (~15,000 runners); warmest October on record since 1884, cooler weather next week; One Marina flat sale oversubscribed ~33×.
Why it matters to you
Follow-through on the Tai Po fire report is the city’s biggest housing-safety accountability fight in years.

China

Deep dive: APEC Shenzhen and a fragile trade calm

APEC
Leaders’ meeting is Nov 18–19 in Shenzhen (China’s APEC year). Chinese coverage on Nov 1 framed ~300 related events and priorities around openness, innovation and cooperation.
Trade truce
The Guardian (Nov 1) says the US–China trade war looks quieter but the calm is misleading. Other trackers note a truce extended toward Jan 10, 2027, while some rare-earth and export-control legal deadlines still land in early November unless renewed.
Why it matters to you
Tariffs and flight capacity still shape prices and family travel options. Shenzhen week is the next big Xi–Trump-era diplomacy checkpoint.
Source check
