Brooklyn jury convicts Huawei of stealing U.S. trade secrets and bank fraud but acquits it of racketeering; Beijing condemns “suppression”

Official statementAllegationBBC News, The Guardian and 1 more · Nov 24 (verdict); Beijing’s response Nov 25

A federal jury in Brooklyn on Tuesday, Nov. 24, found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud and conspiracy to violate U.S. sanctions on Iran, but acquitted the Chinese tech giant of the central racketeering charge.

Why it matters · Donna Daily analysisThis is the most significant U.S. criminal verdict yet against a major Chinese tech company, and it lands a week after the Trump–Xi summit and alongside reports of new U.S. tariffs. A conviction adds legal weight to Washington’s long-running case against Huawei’s equipment. Experts quoted by the Guardian say the case has not undermined Huawei’s business, though, so the bigger effects may be diplomatic rather than commercial.
Full story, sources & checks3 sources

A federal jury in Brooklyn on Tuesday, Nov. 24, found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud and conspiracy to violate U.S. sanctions on Iran, but acquitted the Chinese tech giant of the central racketeering charge. The racketeering count would have required jurors to find that Huawei acted as a criminal enterprise; it was also cleared of obstruction of justice, according to AFP. Prosecutors had accused Huawei and three subsidiaries of spending two decades lying to banks about its business in Iran and stealing technology from rivals. “No person or company is above the law,” Attorney General Todd Blanche said. Huawei said the acquittal “confirms that these allegations lack factual basis” and called the remaining convictions “without legal or factual merit”, promising “all necessary legal actions”. Its lawyer told the BBC he expects an appeal. Sentencing is planned for September 2027. China’s foreign ministry said on Wednesday it “firmly opposes the US suppressing and containing Chinese enterprises.” The case dates to 2018, when Huawei finance chief Meng Wanzhou was arrested in Canada, setting off a three-way diplomatic crisis.

Note: Huawei can appeal; convictions are not final until sentencing and any appeals. Sources differ on trial length (“two-month” per the BBC; “more than two months” per the Guardian; “three-month” per AFP via HKFP).

Michael Barnhart, a former U.S. Army intelligence officer who investigates North Korea, said Zhang was already known to researchers. The B.C. government did not respond, and in reply to a freedom of information request the ministry said it could not locate an employee by that name.
